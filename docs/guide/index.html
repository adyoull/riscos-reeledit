Introduction

So you've got some video (holiday clips from your phone, a recording of the school play, footage for a club website) and you'd like to turn it into something people will actually want to watch. On RISC OS. Good news: Reel Edit does exactly that, and this guide shows you how, one friendly step at a time.

About this guide

This guide comes with Reel Edit: it's !ReelEdit.Guide.index/html. Shift-double-click !ReelEdit to open the application directory, open Guide, and double-click index/html; NetSurf shows it, pictures and all.

You don't have to read this guide from front to back. Each chapter stands on its own. If you only want to add a title to a clip, jump to Chapter 12. If you've never edited video before, start at Part I and work through the Try it examples; by the end of Part II you'll have made your first edit.

Every picture in this guide is a real screenshot of Reel Edit running on RISC OS, taken while the guide was being written. The test clips are colour bars with a moving line, so you can see exactly which frame is which.

Foolish assumptions

We assume you've used RISC OS a little: you know that the middle mouse button is Menu, the left one is Select and the right one is Adjust, and you can drag a file from a directory display (a Filer window) into a program. That's all. You don't need to know anything about video.

Part I: Getting StartedInstall it, open it, and learn your way around

1 Meet Reel Edit

In this chapter: what Reel Edit is, what it can do, and what you need to run it.

Reel Edit is a non-linear video editor. "Non-linear" just means you can work on any part of your film in any order: chop the end off one clip, slot another into the middle, add music under the lot, and change your mind as often as you like. Nothing happens to your original video files. Reel Edit keeps a project, a list of which bits of which files go where, and only makes a new video file when you export.

What it can do

  • Cut and arrange: split clips, trim their ends, move them about, delete what you don't want, with unlimited Undo (well, the last 100 edits).
  • Transitions: fade from and to black, and dissolve one clip into the next.
  • Sound: mute a clip, fade its sound, change its volume, and add music and sound effects on audio tracks that stack up as you add more.
  • Overlays: put a logo in the corner, show one video inside another (picture in picture), or lay a picture over the whole frame.
  • Titles: type text and place it over the video, with a choice of fonts, colours and styles.
  • Speed: slow motion, fast forward and reverse.
  • Professional tools: marks, three-point editing from a Clips window, rolling edits, copy and paste.
  • Smooth playback: render previews and automatic proxies (small working copies of big files), so busy projects play smoothly even on a Raspberry Pi.
  • Export to an MP4 file that plays everywhere, using the Raspberry Pi's VideoCore hardware encoder when it's there.

What you need

ThingWhy
RISC OS 5 on an ARMv7 machine with VFP: a Raspberry Pi 2, 3 or 4Reel Edit is built for these processors.
SharedUnixLibrary 1.16 or later, and ARMEABISupportLibraries Reel Edit is built on. Install them with PackMan. !Run checks for them and tells you if they're missing.
SharedSoundBuffer and StreamManager (ssb.zip), plus SharedSound (part of RISC OS)To hear the sound. Without them, you still see the pictures.
The VideoOverlay module (in !System on RISC OS 5)To show the picture through the Pi's video hardware while playing. Without it, the picture is drawn in the window instead, which is slower.

TECHNICAL STUFFReel Edit is built on MLT, the same multimedia framework used by Shotcut and Kdenlive on other systems, and on FFmpeg for reading and writing video. Projects are saved as MLT XML, so in principle Shotcut can open them too. H.264 video is decoded by the Pi's VideoCore through riscos-reelhwaccel, and HEVC by the Pi 4's HEVC block; anything they can't decode is decoded by the ARM.

2 Installing and starting

In this chapter: putting Reel Edit on your disc and opening the editor.

  1. Unzip ReelEdit-0.3-test28.zip with SparkFS or !Unzip, and copy !ReelEdit to wherever you keep your programs (Apps or $.Apps.Video are good places).
  2. Double-click !ReelEdit. Its icon appears on the icon bar, at the bottom right of the screen (the round film reel in the picture below).
  3. Click the icon. The editor opens with a new, empty project.
REMEMBERMenu over the icon bar icon gives Info (which version you have) and Quit. If you have unsaved changes, Reel Edit asks before it lets anything go.
WARNINGIf !Run stops with "Reel Edit needs SharedUnixLibrary 1.16 or later" or "needs the ARMEABISupport module", install those with PackMan first. Reel Edit won't start without them.

3 A tour of the screen

In this chapter: the Viewer, the Timeline, and the menus. Five minutes here saves an hour of puzzling later.

The Viewer

The top window is the Viewer: it shows the frame at the playhead (the current position). Under the picture is the transport:

ButtonDoes
|<Goes to the start.
<= and =>Step back or forward one frame (click with Adjust to step a whole second).
PlayPlays, or stops if it's playing. The Space bar does the same.
=>|Goes to the end.
The barShows where you are in the project. Click or drag in it to jump.
The timecodeHours:minutes:seconds:frames at the playhead, and the project's length.

Under the transport is a toolbar: Split, Delete, Undo, Redo, Snap, the Timeline zoom buttons (-, +, Fit) and Save. Each one has interactive help: switch on !Help and hover over it.

The Timeline

The lower window is the Timeline, where your film is laid out from left to right. From the top:

  • The ruler shows time. Click it to move the playhead (the red line); drag along it to scrub, which shows the frames as you go. Under the ruler, a thin coloured bar shows render previews (Chapter 15).
  • V2 is the overlay track: logos, titles and picture-in-picture videos sit here, over the main video.
  • V1 is the main video track: your clips, one after another. Each clip shows its first frame (and its last, if it's wide enough), its name, and a light-blue waveform of its sound along the bottom.
  • Audio tracks (A1, A2 and so on) appear below V1 as soon as you add music or sound effects. There's always one empty row at the bottom to drop more onto.

The menus

Press Menu over the Viewer or the Timeline's ruler for Reel Edit's main menu. Press Menu over a clip, an overlay or a sound on the Timeline for a menu about that one thing (the context menu).

TIPEvery menu command has a key, shown beside it in the menu. Learn a handful (Chapter 18 lists the ten most useful) and you'll edit twice as fast.
TECHNICAL STUFFThe Timeline normally moves with the Viewer as one workspace. Untick View > Attach the Timeline to move it independently. View > Viewer size (Small, Medium, Large) sets the Viewer to 35%, 50% or 65% of the screen's width. On the Pi, the picture is scaled by the GPU while playing, so a bigger Viewer costs screen space, not speed.

4 Adding clips

In this chapter: the three ways of getting video into your project, and what files Reel Edit understands.

To add a clip, drag a video file from a directory display to one of three places:

Drop it on……and it goes
The ViewerAt the end of the project.
The icon bar iconAt the end of the project (and the editor opens if it wasn't).
The Timeline's V1 rowWhere you dropped it: before the clip you dropped it on, or after it if you dropped it nearer that clip's end.
TRY ITYour first two-clip project.
  1. Click Reel Edit's icon to open an empty project.
  2. Open the directory holding your videos, and drag one onto the Viewer. It appears on V1 and its first frame shows in the Viewer.
  3. Drag a second video onto the Viewer. It goes after the first.
  4. Press Space to play it all. Press Space again to stop.
That's an edit! The picture in Chapter 3 shows what you should see.

What can I drop?

  • Video files FFmpeg can read: MP4 and MOV (H.264, HEVC), MKV, WebM, AVI, MPEG and more. H.264 up to 1080p is decoded by the Pi's VideoCore; the rest by the ARM, with a proxy made for you (Chapter 15).
  • Pictures: PNG, JPEG and sprite files. Dropped on V1, a picture becomes a 5-second clip; dropped on V2, it becomes an overlay (Chapter 11).
  • Sound files: WAV, MP3, AAC, Ogg and FLAC. Drop these on the audio rows (Chapter 10).
  • A project: an MLT XML file (from Reel Edit, Shotcut, Kdenlive or melt) is opened as the project.
TECHNICAL STUFFRaw video streams that don't say how long they are (.m2v MPEG-2, raw H.264) are first read through to measure them. "Measuring" and its progress show by the transport while the desktop carries on, and the clip goes in when it's done. Escape stops it. Any files you drop in the meantime wait their turn.

6 Cutting, trimming and moving

In this chapter: the bread and butter of editing. Split, trim, move, delete, and roll.

Selecting

Click a clip to select it: it gets an orange outline and orange handles at its ends. Ctrl-Z clears the selection.

Splitting

Put the playhead where you want to cut and press S (or click Split). The clip becomes two clips, which you can now trim, move or delete separately.

Trimming

There are three ways to trim:

  • Drag a clip's edge. Drag near its left or right edge, and the Viewer shows the frame at the edge as you go. Everything after it moves along to keep the film continuous (a ripple trim).
  • Q trims the clip at the playhead so that it starts at the playhead (the frames before go).
  • W trims it so that it ends just before the playhead (the rest goes).
TIPQ and W are the fastest way to tidy up a clip: play it, stop where the good bit starts, press Q; play on, stop where it ends, press W. Two keys and it's trimmed.

Moving

Drag a clip by its middle. A white line shows where it will go (at the nearest cut), and it goes there when you let go.

Deleting

Delete (or Delete on the toolbar) removes the selected clip and closes the gap. Shift-Delete (or clicking Delete with Adjust) leaves a gap of black instead.

Rolling a cut

Sometimes you like both clips but want the cut between them a little earlier or later. That's a roll: one clip gets longer by exactly as much as the other gets shorter, so nothing else moves.

  • Put the playhead where you want the cut and press E: the nearest cut rolls to the playhead.
  • Or hold Ctrl and drag a clip's edge: the cut there rolls as you drag.
REMEMBERA plain drag on an edge is a ripple trim (the film gets longer or shorter). A Ctrl-drag is a roll (the film stays the same length). A clip can only roll as far as its file has frames to give.

Undo and Redo

F8 undoes and F9 redoes, up to 100 edits back. Each drag counts as a single edit, however far you dragged.

TRY ITTighten up a clip. Play your first clip and stop where the interesting part starts. Press Q. Play on to where it gets boring, stop, and press W. Then press F8 twice to see it back as it was, and F9 twice to put your trims back.

7 Marks, extract and lift

In this chapter: marking a stretch of the project and acting on all of it at once.

Press I to mark the in point at the playhead and O to mark the out point. The marked stretch shows in pale blue on the ruler, with blue lines down through the track.

KeyDoes
I / OMark in / out at the playhead.
Shift-I / Shift-OGo to the in / out mark.
ReturnPlay from in to out, and stop.
' (Extract)Take out the marked stretch and close the gap.
; (Lift)Take out the marked stretch and leave a gap.
Play > Clear the marksRemove both marks.
TIPExtract is perfect for "cut out the bit where the dog walks in front of the camera": mark in before it, mark out after it, press '. Reel Edit splits the clips at both marks for you.

8 Cut, Copy and Paste

In this chapter: duplicating and moving things with the clipboard.

Select a clip, an overlay or a sound, then:

  • Ctrl-C copies it, with its fades, mute, volume and (for an overlay) its place, size and opacity;
  • Ctrl-X cuts it (copies it, then deletes it);
  • Ctrl-V pastes it at the playhead. A clip is inserted there and what follows moves along; an overlay goes on V2 where there's room; a sound goes on the audio tracks.

The pasted item is selected and the playhead jumps to its end, so pressing Ctrl-V again puts another copy straight after it.

TRY ITRepeat a title three times. Make a title (Chapter 12), select it on V2 and press Ctrl-C. Move the playhead and press Ctrl-V three times. Each copy is a title of its own: change the text of one and the others stay as they were.

Part III: Making It Look and Sound GoodTransitions, sound, overlays, titles and speed

9 Fades and dissolves

In this chapter: the two transitions that cover nine edits out of ten.

Press Menu over a clip to get its context menu:

Fades

Fade in fades the clip up from black over its first second; Fade out fades it to black over its last second. The sound fades with the picture. Each one is ticked when the clip has it; choose it again to remove it. The fade appears as a purple box along the top of the clip. Drag the box's white inner edge to make the fade longer or shorter.

Dissolves

A dissolve (or crossfade) blends the end of one clip into the start of the next. Make one by:

  • pressing D: a one-second dissolve at the cut nearest the playhead;
  • choosing Dissolve from the clip before or Dissolve into the clip after from a clip's menu;
  • or dragging the little white triangle at the bottom of a cut: the dissolve is as long as you drag.

Drag either end of a dissolve to lengthen or shorten it. To remove it, press Menu over it and choose Delete; the clips go back to how they were.

TECHNICAL STUFFA dissolve uses frames from beyond the cut when the clips have them (the first clip's frames after its out point, or the second's before its in point), so the project keeps its length. When they don't, the clips are overlapped and the project gets shorter by the dissolve's length.

10 Sound

In this chapter: a clip's own sound, and music and effects on audio tracks.

A clip's sound

The Sound submenu of a clip's menu lets you work on its sound alone:

  • Mute silences the clip. A red line along its bottom shows it.
  • Fade in and Fade out fade the sound alone over a second, leaving the picture as it is. They show as green wedges.
  • Volume sets the level from +12 dB to −36 dB. The level shows after the clip's name, for example "(−6 dB)".
TIPUsing a video as picture in picture but don't want its sound? Mute it. Want the music to dip while someone talks? Give the music −12 dB.

Audio tracks: music and sound effects

Drop a sound file (WAV, MP3, AAC, Ogg or FLAC), or a video whose sound you want, onto the audio row below V1. It goes on track A1 at the point where you dropped it. Drop another where one is already playing and it goes on the track below, so sound effects stack. New tracks are added as needed.

Drag a sound by its middle to move it and by its ends to trim it. Press Menu over it for its own menu:

REMEMBERThe waveforms are drawn as you'll hear them: at the clip's volume and through its fades. A muted clip shows no waveform. If a waveform looks flat, the sound really is quiet.

11 Logos, overlays and picture in picture

In this chapter: putting one thing over another.

Anything on the V2 row is shown over the video on V1. Drop a picture (PNG with transparency, JPEG, or a sprite file) or a video on the V2 row, where you want it to start.

  • A picture as big as the frame (or near it) fills the frame; a smaller one becomes a logo in the top right. It lasts 5 seconds; drag its ends to change that.
  • A video becomes a picture in the picture, in the bottom right at a third of the size, and its sound mixes with V1's.
  • A sprite file with more than one sprite gives you a menu to pick from. The sprite is saved as a PNG beside the file.

Press Menu over an overlay for its menu:

13 Speed and reverse

In this chapter: slow motion, fast forward and backwards.

Press Menu over a clip and open Speed:

  • Choose a speed: 200% plays twice as fast (the clip becomes half as long), 50% is slow motion (it becomes twice as long). The clip keeps its start; what follows moves along.
  • Reversed plays the clip backwards. Choose it again to play forwards. A reversed clip stays reversed when you pick another speed.
  • The clip's name shows its speed, for example "v1.mp4 50%" or "v1.mp4 reversed".
WARNINGThe sound changes pitch with the speed: at 50% it's an octave lower, at 200% an octave higher. If that sounds silly, mute the clip (Sound > Mute) and add music instead.
REMEMBERPictures (they have no speed) and clips with a dissolve at either end can't have their speed changed. Take the dissolve out first, change the speed, then put the dissolve back.

Part IV: Working Like a ProFaster editing, smoother playback, safe saving and the finished film

14 The Clips window and three-point editing

In this chapter: keeping clips to hand, and editing parts of them straight into your film.

Press Ctrl-B (or View > Clips…) to open the Clips window, which lists every file in your project, plus any files you drop on the window itself. It's saved with the project.

  • Drag a clip from the list to the Timeline to put the whole of it there.
  • Double-click a clip (or select it and press Return) to show it in the Viewer as a source.
  • Delete takes a clip off the list (it stays on the Timeline).

Three-point editing

With a source showing, the Viewer's title says "Source:", and the keys work on the source:

  1. Play the source and press I and O to mark the part you want.
  2. Press , (comma) to insert that part at the project's playhead: what's there moves along.
  3. Or press . (full stop) to overwrite: it replaces what's at the playhead for its length.
  4. The playhead moves to just after the new piece, so you can mark the next part and put it straight after.
  5. Press Escape to see the project again.
TIPThis is how professionals assemble a rough cut quickly: mark a good moment in a long recording, comma, mark the next, comma… and the film builds itself in order on the Timeline.

15 Smooth playback: render previews and proxies

In this chapter: two features that make busy projects play smoothly, and when to use each.

Why playback sometimes stutters

Showing a plain clip means decoding one picture per frame, which the Pi's VideoCore does in about 10 ms at 1080p. Picture in picture means decoding two pictures and combining them, and the Pi only gives Reel Edit one VideoCore decoder, so the second is decoded on the ARM at about 43 ms. That's more than the 40 ms a frame has at 25 frames a second. Fades, dissolves, titles and speed changes add work too. Reel Edit keeps the sound and the picture in time by skipping pictures it can't make in time, so a busy stretch looks jerky.

Render previews

Press Ctrl-R (or Play > Render previews) and Reel Edit makes each busy stretch into a ready-made video file in the background. When you play, those stretches play from their files: one decode, no combining. A bar under the ruler shows each stretch's state:

  • Busy stretches are those with an overlay or title, a dissolve, a fade, or a clip at another speed. Stretches less than 2 seconds apart are rendered as one.
  • You can keep editing while it renders. Play > Stop rendering stops it.
  • Change anything in a stretch and it goes red again until you press Ctrl-R. Moving a stretch along with a ripple edit keeps its preview.

Proxies

Some files are hard work all the time: HEVC (H.265, what most phones record), VP9, 4K, or anything else the VideoCore can't decode. For these, Reel Edit makes a proxy for you automatically: a small 360-line copy with a keyframe every 5 frames, which decodes quickly and seeks instantly. While they're being made, the Timeline's title says so:

  • A proxy is used in the Viewer and on the Timeline as soon as it's ready. Clips keep their own names.
  • Saving, exporting, render previews and autosave always use your original files, at full quality.
  • View > Use proxies turns them off and on.
  • Proxies are deleted when you close the editor or quit. They're kept only to recover a project after a crash (Chapter 16).

How much do they help?

Measured on a slow test machine without a VideoCore, playing 1080p sources:

CaseWithout proxiesWith proxies
One 1080p H.264 clip2.4 pictures/s9.7 pictures/s
1080p with a 1080p picture in picture2.6 pictures/s4.4 pictures/s
One 1080p HEVC clip1.9 pictures/s6.3 pictures/s
REMEMBEROn a Pi, plain 1080p H.264 already plays smoothly through the VideoCore, so proxies help most with HEVC, 4K and picture in picture. Render previews help with anything busy. They work well together.

16 Saving, autosave and recovery

In this chapter: keeping your work safe.

Press F3 or click Save. The first time, a Save as box appears; drag its icon to a directory display, as in any RISC OS program. After that, F3 saves to the same file. File > Save as… saves under a new name.

Projects are saved as MLT XML files (filetype XML). The Viewer's title shows the file's name, with a star (*) when there are changes not saved yet.

Autosave

While you have unsaved changes, Reel Edit keeps a safety copy of the project in <Choices$Write>.ReelEdit.Autosave (normally inside !Boot.Choices), updated at most every half minute as you work. The copy is removed when you save, close the editor or quit, so a copy that's still there means Reel Edit stopped unexpectedly (a crash, Ctrl-Break, a power cut).

Recovery

Next time you open the editor, Reel Edit offers to recover it:

  • Recover opens the project as it was, still marked as needing to be saved. Proxies already made for it are reused.
  • Discard throws the copy away, together with any proxies and render previews the stopped session left behind.
WARNINGAutosave is a safety net, not a substitute for saving. Press F3 often, especially before exporting or trying something drastic.

17 Exporting your film

In this chapter: making the finished video file.

Press Ctrl-E (or File > Export…):

SettingChoices
SizeThe project's, 1080 lines, 720 lines, 480 lines or 360 lines (the shape is kept).
QualitySmall file, Normal or High.
VideoCore encoderUses the Pi's hardware encoder: several times faster than the ARM. Greyed out when it isn't available.

Drag the file icon to a directory display, or type a full file name and click Export. The export runs in the background (you can keep editing) and the window shows its progress. Stop cancels it and deletes the part-made file.

TECHNICAL STUFFThe file is an MP4 with H.264 video and AAC sound (48 kHz stereo), with its index at the start so it streams. The encoder is the VideoCore's (h264_encvchiq) when ticked, otherwise libx264, otherwise MPEG-4 part 2. Bit rates are 0.07, 0.10 or 0.15 bits per pixel for the three qualities. The export is made by Reel Edit's own program running in a TaskWindow, from a copy of the project, so you can't spoil it by editing meanwhile.

18 Ten shortcuts worth learning first

  1. Space: play and stop.
  2. J K L: backwards, stop, forwards (press again to go faster).
  3. Left / Right: a frame at a time; with Shift, a second.
  4. Up / Down: jump to the previous / next cut.
  5. S: split at the playhead.
  6. Q / W: trim the clip's start / end to the playhead.
  7. Delete: delete and close the gap.
  8. D: dissolve at the nearest cut.
  9. F8 / F9: undo / redo.
  10. F3: save.

20 Ten things for advanced users

  1. Projects are MLT XML. Reel Edit saves Shotcut's track layout (V1 a playlist; V2 and the audio tracks marked as Shotcut marks its own) with store=shotcut properties, so the files are readable by melt and, in principle, Shotcut. Reel Edit's own settings (overlay place, size, opacity, fades; the Clips list) are stored as shotcut:reeledit_* properties.
  2. The log. <Wimp$ScrapDir>.ReelEditLog records every edit, the decoder used for each clip, playback statistics each second ("frame N: pictures, left out, slowed…" and "time a picture: …"), render and proxy jobs. Set the system variable ReelEdit$Log to a file name to put it elsewhere, or to off.
  3. Background jobs are TaskWindows. Exports, render previews and proxies are made by !RunImage -export or -proxy running in a TaskWindow, which RISC OS multitasks pre-emptively. That's why the desktop stays responsive while they run.
  4. Render signatures. Each busy stretch is identified by a hash of everything in it (clips, overlays, sounds, their frames and settings), measured from its start. A preview is reused as long as that hash is unchanged, which is why a ripple edit earlier in the project doesn't throw previews away.
  5. Proxies are swapped in the XML. When a proxy is ready, the project is rebuilt from its XML with the file's name replaced by the proxy's, and undo and redo do the same. Saving swaps them back, so saved projects always name the original files.
  6. Where the files go. Render previews are kept in <Wimp$ScrapDir>.ReelEdPrv; proxies are in <Wimp$ScrapDir>.ReelEdPxy, with an Index file that maps each proxy to its source. Both are deleted on closing or quitting, and on start-up unless a project is recovered.
  7. One VideoCore decoder. With gpu_mem=128, a second h264_vchiq decoder opens but never gives a picture, so Reel Edit keeps one and hands it from clip to clip. In a dissolve or picture in picture, the second stream is decoded on the ARM, which is where render previews and proxies earn their keep.
  8. Thumbnails and waveforms are made with FFmpeg's software decoders (leaving the VideoCore free for playing), a few hundredths of a second at a time on idle null events, what's in view first. The waveform's scale is in dB, from 48 dB below full scale up to full scale.
  9. Speeds are MLT timewarp producers (timewarp:2:file), as Shotcut makes them, created through MLT's loader so their sound is resampled to 48 kHz. Pitch correction needs the Rubber Band library, which isn't built in.
  10. Interactive help everywhere. Run !Help and hover over any button, track or window part: Reel Edit explains what it does and which keys do the same.
